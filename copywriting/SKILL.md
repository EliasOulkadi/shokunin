Write marketing copy that converts readers into customers. Based on direct response research from Claude Hopkins, John Caples, Eugene Schwartz, and modern analysts like Joanna Wiebe, Neville Medhora, and the Copyhackers team.

## Frameworks

| Framework | Structure | Best for |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| AIDA | Attention -> Interest -> Desire -> Action | Landing pages, emails, general sales copy |
| PAS | Problem -> Agitate -> Solve | Pain-point focused sales pages, email sequences |
| BAB | Before -> After -> Bridge | Transformational offers, coaching, services |
| FAB | Feature -> Advantage -> Benefit | Product descriptions, feature announcements |
| 4Ps | Picture -> Promise -> Prove -> Push | Direct response, VSLs, long-form |
| AICPBSAWN | Awareness -> Interest -> Comprehension -> Persuasion -> Bridge -> Scarcity -> Action -> Warn -> Now | Long-form landing pages, high-ticket sales |
| QUEST | Qualify -> Understand -> Educate -> Stimulate -> Transition | B2B, complex sales, consultative |
| SLAP | Stop -> Look -> Act -> Purchase | Email subject lines, PPC ads |

## Tone & Voice Matrix

| Brand type | Voice | Vocabulary | Sentence length |
|------------|-------|------------|-----------------|
| Premium luxury | Authoritative, elevated | Precise, exclusive, sensory | Medium (15-20 words) |
| Friendly B2C | Warm, conversational | Contractions, everyday language | Short (10-15 words) |
| B2B SaaS | Direct, benefit-focused | Industry terms explained, data-driven | Medium-short |
| Disruptive startup | Bold, challenging | Short, punchy, occasional slang | Very short (5-10 words) |
| Educational | Helpful, authoritative | Clear, jargon-light, pedagogical | Medium |
| Nonprofit | Emotional, urgent | Humanizing, concrete, mission-driven | Short to medium |

## Structure: The 7-Part Sales Page

1. **Headline**: specific benefit, under 15 words, targets one reader. 80% of your result.
2. **Subheadline**: expands the promise, adds specificity, bridges to the body
3. **Opening**: names the problem the reader has — make them feel seen
4. **Body**: shows how your solution fixes it, layer proof throughout
5. **Proof**: testimonials, data, case studies, demonstrations
6. **Objection handling**: address the top 3 reasons they might not buy
7. **CTA**: one clear action, specific outcome, no ambiguity

## Conversion Drivers

| Driver | Psychology | Implementation |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| Specificity | Concrete claims are more believable | "3.2x ROI" not "better results" |
| Social proof | People follow peers | Testimonials near every claim |
| Risk reversal | Loss aversion is stronger than gain | "Money-back guarantee. No forms. No questions." |
| Scarcity | Limited supply triggers action | "12 spots left. 48 hours." (must be genuine) |
| Anchoring | First number sets reference | Show higher price first, then yours |
| Decoy | Unattractive option boosts the offer | 3 pricing tiers, middle is the offer |
| Commitment | Small yes leads to big yes | "Click here to see pricing" → "Start trial" |
| Reciprocity | Give value before asking | Free guide, template, or tool |

## Email Copy Patterns

| Type | Subject line | Body structure |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| Welcome | "Welcome to [brand]" | Value first, pitch second. First 3 emails: no hard sell. |
| Promotional | "[Benefit] — [time limit]" | Problem → solution → proof → CTA |
| Educational | "How to [do thing]" | Teach something useful. Pitch as a PS. |
| Re-engagement | "We miss you" | Offer value or discount. Ask if they want to stay. |
| Abandoned cart | "Your cart is expiring" | Remind what they picked. Social proof. Low-friction CTA. |
| Launch sequence | "[Day N]: [topic]" | Build anticipation day 1-3. Open cart day 4. Close day 5-7. |

## The Copywriting Pre-Flight Checklist

- [ ] Headline passes the "so what?" test — specific benefit to one reader
- [ ] Opening sentence names the reader's problem exactly
- [ ] Every feature is translated to a benefit (feature → advantage → benefit)
- [ ] Social proof placed near the CTA, not buried at the bottom
- [ ] Top 3 objections addressed before the reader reaches them
- [ ] CTA is one action, specific outcome, visible without scrolling
- [ ] No jargon, no passive voice, no weasel words ("might", "could", "potentially")
- [ ] Read aloud: does it sound like a human wrote it?
- [ ] Scannable: short paragraphs, bold key phrases, bulleted benefits
- [ ] Mobile: CTA visible without pinch-zoom, text legible at 16px
- [ ] A/B test planned for headline and CTA

## Anti-Patterns

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Generic headline | No one reads past it | Be specific: name the outcome + audience |
| Feature dumping | Reader does not connect to their life | Convert every feature to a benefit |
| Weak CTA | Low click-through rate | Use action verbs, specific outcome: "Start my free trial" not "Submit" |
| No social proof | Reader does not trust the claim | Add testimonials, numbers, logos near the ask |
| Writing for everyone | Resonates with no one | Define one reader. Write to them. |
| Hiding the CTA | Drop-off before conversion | Repeat CTA every 500 words on long pages |
| Passive voice | Weak, unconvincing | "You get results" not "Results can be achieved" |
| Weasel words | Undermines credibility | "Cut costs by 30%" not "Could help reduce costs" |
| Burying the lead | Reader leaves before finding the offer | State the value proposition in the first 2 paragraphs |
| No proof | Claims look made up | Back every claim with data, testimonial, or demonstration |
