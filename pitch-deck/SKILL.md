Generate pitch deck content that tells a compelling story and raises funding. Based on the 10-slide structure from Sequoia Capital, Y Combinator, and analysis of 500+ venture-backed pitch decks.

## The 10 Slides

1. **Title**: company name, tagline (problem or vision in one line), founder name
2. **Problem**: what's broken, who feels it, how bad is it, why now
3. **Solution**: your product, simply stated. Show, don't tell.
4. **Market**: TAM, SAM, SOM with sources and assumptions
5. **Product**: demo, screenshots, key workflow. NOT a feature list.
6. **Traction**: revenue, users, growth rate, retention, engagement
7. **Business model**: how you make money, unit economics, pricing
8. **Competition**: landscape map (2x2 matrix), your moat, barriers
9. **Team**: relevant experience, domain expertise, why this team wins
10. **Ask**: how much, what it funds, milestones for next 18 months

## Story Arc

| Slide | Emotion it should evoke | Question it answers for the investor |
|-------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| 1-2 | Pain, recognition | Why should I care? Is this a real problem? |
| 3-4 | Hope, ambition | Is the solution compelling? Is the market big enough? |
| 5-6 | Excitement, proof | Does it work? Is anyone using it? |
| 7-8 | Confidence, trust | Does the business make sense? Can they win? |
| 9-10 | Action, urgency | Is this the right team? Why now? |

## Content Rules

| Rule | Why | Exception |
|------|-----|-----------|
| One slide, one idea | Multi-idea slides confuse. No one remembers more than one thing per slide. | None |
| Maximum 20 words per slide (slides 1-4, 7-10) | Slides are billboards, not documents. Short text forces clarity. | Slides 5-6 (demo, traction) can have more detail |
| No bullet points | Bullets = "I didn't have time to figure out what to say." Use short phrases. | Data tables in traction slide |
| Font minimum: 24pt content, 36pt headlines | Investors review on phones. If they can't read it, it doesn't exist. | Labels on charts |
| Images > text for product slides | A screenshot of the product is worth 100 words of description. | When product isn't visual (API, infrastructure) |
| 3 seconds per slide max | Investor spends 3 minutes on a deck = 30 slides viewed + discussed. Trim aggressively. | Traction and ask slides get more time |

## Slide Details

### Problem Slide
Must convince the investor that this is:
- A real problem (not a nice-to-have)
- Widespread (not niche)
- Urgent (why now?)
- Growing (getting worse, not better)
- Painful enough to pay for

Format: "Before [solution], [audience] had to [painful workaround]. This cost them [specific bad outcome]."

### Solution Slide
One sentence: "We help [customer] do [X] so they can [Y]."
Then a 3-step visual of how it works. Show the input → process → output.

### Market Slide
| Metric | Definition | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| TAM (Total Addressable Market) | Total revenue opportunity if 100% market share | $50B global |
| SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market) | Segment you can reach with your product | $10B |
| SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market) | Realistic near-term target | $500M in 3 years |

Always cite sources. Investors verify.

### Traction Slide
Investor expectations by stage:

| Stage | Key metric | Benchmark | What they're proving |
|-------|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Pre-seed | Problem validation | 50+ user interviews, waitlist of 500+ | People want this |
| Seed | Engagement | 10k+ MAU, 30%+ weekly retention | People use this |
| Series A | Revenue | $1M+ ARR, < 20% monthly churn | People pay for this |
| Series B | Growth + unit economics | 3x YoY, > 80% gross retention, LTV/CAC > 3 | This is a business |

Show the growth curve. Month-over-month or quarter-over-quarter. Never show a flat line.

### Competition Slide

Use a 2x2 matrix:
- X-axis: feature depth (narrow → full platform)
- Y-axis: target market (consumer → enterprise)
- Place competitors as circles sized by revenue/user count
- Label YOUR winning quadrant
- Show your position as unique, not crowded

Then list your moat:
- Technology: proprietary, hard to replicate
- Network effects: each user makes the product better
- Brand: trust, recognition
- Data: unique dataset that improves with scale
- Distribution: channel partnerships, embedded sales

### Team Slide

| What to include | What to avoid |
|----------------|---------------|
| Relevant experience: "Built X at Y, grew to Z users" | Generic bios: "10 years of experience" |
| Previous exits or successful products | Degrees alone (unless exceptional) |
| Why THIS team: complementary skills that match the problem | "We've known each other for 10 years" without context |
| 2-3 founders max | Full org chart — that's for later |

Investors bet on teams, not ideas. The team slide is the most important slide after traction.

### Ask Slide

```
We're raising [$X] at [$Y valuation]

Use of funds:
→ [%] Engineering: product, platform, infrastructure
→ [%] Go-to-market: sales, marketing, partnerships
→ [%] Operations: support, admin, compliance
→ [%] Reserve: 6-month runway buffer

Milestones for next 18 months:
→ [Metric 1]: from [current] to [target]
→ [Metric 2]: from [current] to [target]
→ Team: from [N] to [N] people
→ Runway: through [month, year]
```

## Pitch Deck Design Rules

- **Dark text on light background**. Light text on dark background is harder to read in a bright room.
- **One font family**: 1-2 weights max. Serif for headlines if premium, sans for everything else.
- **Consistent layout**: same margin, same title position on every slide.
- **Images at full resolution**: pixelated screenshots signal low quality.
- **No slide numbers on first and last slides**: they create a "countdown" feeling.
- **PDF format**: always send as PDF, not PowerPoint. 5MB max file size.

## Pitch Deck Mistakes That Kill Deals

| Mistake | Why it kills |
|---------|-------------|
| Too many words | Investor stops reading. You lose the room. |
| No traction | "We just launched" is not traction. Show something. |
| Market too small | Investors need to believe in a billion-dollar outcome. |
| No clear ask | "We're raising a round" without specifics. How much? What for? |
| Over-engineered financials | 5-year projections for a pre-revenue company = laughable. 12-18 months is credible. |
| Hiding the competition | Investor will find them anyway. Show you know the landscape. |
| No customer names | "We have paying customers" without names = no credibility. |
| Bad design | Looks amateur = feels amateur. First impression matters. |
| Too many slides | More slides = less time per slide = less impact. 10-12 slides max. |
| No "why now" | Markets shift. Why is this the right moment for this company? |
