Run structured brainstorming sessions that produce actionable ideas. Alternates divergent and convergent thinking for maximum output without chaos. Based on IDEO design thinking, Alex Osborn's original method, and modern facilitation research from the d.school, Mural, and SessionLab.

## The Process

Round 1: Divergent (generate, no judgment) — 15-30 min
Round 2: Clustering (organize, group, eliminate) — 10-15 min
Round 3: Convergent (select, vote, prioritize, commit) — 15-20 min

Each round is separate. Never mix divergent and convergent — judgment kills ideas before they form.

## Divergent Techniques

| Technique | How | Best for |
|-----------|-----|----------|
| Free association | Write everything that comes to mind, no filter | Warm-up, quantity generation |
| Brainwriting 6-3-5 | 6 people write 3 ideas in 5 min, pass to next | Avoiding dominant voices, maximum participation |
| Reverse brainstorming | How would we make the problem worse? Then reverse the answers | Breaking assumptions, fresh angles |
| Random word | Pick a random noun, force connections to the problem | Breaking mental blocks, lateral thinking |
| SCAMPER | Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to use, Eliminate, Reverse | Systematic exploration of a product or service |
| How Might We (HMW) | Reframe problem as opportunity: "HMW [desired outcome]?" | Design thinking, user-centered problems |
| Worst idea | Generate terrible ideas intentionally, then invert them | Reducing fear, unlocking creativity |
| Mind mapping | Start with central concept, branch outward freely | Visual thinkers, non-linear problems |
| Body storming | Act out scenarios physically | Physical products, spatial problems |
| Analogy thinking | Compare problem to unrelated domain | Breakthrough concepts, novel solutions |

## Clustering Techniques

| Technique | How | Best for |
|-----------|-----|----------|
| Affinity mapping | Group ideas by theme on a wall or board | Large sets of ideas (50+) |
| Theme finding | Label each cluster with a descriptive header | Identifying patterns and categories |
| Outlier surfacing | Identify ideas that don't fit any group | Potentially breakthrough concepts |
| Combine and merge | Merge similar ideas into stronger ones | Reducing noise, strengthening concepts |

## Convergent Techniques

| Technique | How | Best for |
|-----------|-----|----------|
| Dot voting | Each person gets 3-5 votes, place on ideas | Quick prioritization |
| Impact/effort matrix | X: impact, Y: effort. Plot each idea. | Deciding what to execute first |
| How-Now-Wow matrix | X: ease of implementation, Y: originality | Finding breakthrough ideas |
| NUF test | Score each idea: New, Useful, Feasible (1-10 each) | Objective filtering |
| ICE scoring | Impact, Confidence, Ease (1-10 each, average) | Prioritizing growth experiments |
| Bingo selection | Create a 5x5 grid of ideas, play bingo with criteria | Making selection fun and participative |
| 5 Whys | For top ideas: ask "why" 5 times to test robustness | Stress-testing selected concepts |

## Session Rules

### Ground Rules (share at the start)

| Rule | Why | How to enforce |
|------|-----|----------------|
| Defer judgment | Judgment kills ideas before they form | No "but", "that won't work", "we tried that" |
| Go for quantity | Quantity breeds quality — more ideas = more chance of a great one | Set explicit targets: "50 ideas in 20 min" |
| One conversation at a time | Side conversations fragment the group energy | Use a talking stick or physical token |
| Be visual | Drawings, diagrams, stickies communicate faster than words | Provide markers, stickies, whiteboard |
| Build on others | "Yes, and..." not "Yes, but..." | Explicitly teach the improv rule |
| Stay on topic | Focus on the problem statement | Write the problem on the wall, point to it |
| Encourage wild ideas | Safe ideas are forgettable. Wild ideas spark new thinking | Reward the most unexpected idea |
| Share the air | Ensure everyone speaks, not just the loudest voices | Round-robin, brainwriting, timeboxed turns |

### Facilitator Rules

- Do NOT participate in idea generation — your job is to manage the process
- Call out rule violations immediately but kindly: "Let's defer judgment — capture that for later"
- Timebox every activity visibly. Use a timer projected on the wall.
- Capture everything — even "bad" ideas can spark good ones
- Protect the introverts: brainwriting before discussion ensures their ideas are heard
- End on time. Running over drains energy. Better to leave them wanting more.

## Session Structure Template

| Phase | Duration | Activity |
|-------|----------|----------|
| Problem framing | 10 min | Restate the problem. "How might we..." question defined. |
| Warm-up | 5 min | Low-stakes exercise (e.g., "How many uses for a paperclip?") |
| Divergent round 1 | 15 min | Brainwriting or free association. Target: 30+ ideas. |
| Cluster | 10 min | Affinity mapping. Label groups. |
| Divergent round 2 | 10 min | SCAMPER or random word on the strongest clusters |
| Convergent | 15 min | Impact/effort matrix or ICE scoring. Select top 3-5. |
| Action planning | 10 min | For each selected idea: owner, next step, deadline |
| Total | 75 min | Ideal session length |

## Remote Brainstorming

| Challenge | Tool | Technique |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| Lack of visual space | Miro, Mural, FigJam | Digital stickies, timer plugins |
| Lag in conversation | Async-first | Google Doc brainwriting over 24h, synchronous vote |
| Low energy | Breakout rooms, physical warm-ups | 5 min break every 25 min, camera-on policy |
| Participation gaps | Round-robin, reaction emojis | "Type your ideas silently for 3 min → share one each" |

## Anti-Patterns

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Mixed divergent and convergent | Judgment kills ideas before they form | Separate phases explicitly with a break |
| Dominant voices take over | Groupthink, missed ideas | Use brainwriting, round-robin |
| Too many people | Chaos, no progress | 4-8 people maximum per session |
| No follow-through | Ideas die after the session | Assign ownership and next steps before closing |
| No warm-up | Participants are cold, ideas are safe | Always do a 5-min warm-up exercise |
| Problem too broad | Ideas are scattered, hard to evaluate | Frame as "How might we..." with clear constraints |
| No visual capture | Ideas get lost or forgotten | Use stickies or digital board, photograph everything |
| Session too long | Energy drops, quality declines | Hard stop at 90 minutes. Multiple short sessions > one marathon. |
| No diverse perspectives | Ideas are homogeneous | Invite people from different roles, departments, backgrounds |
