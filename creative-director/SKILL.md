Generate creative direction across campaigns, products, and brand experiences. Combines systematic innovation methodologies with strategic brief development. Based on frameworks from BBDO, IDEO, Ogilvy, Design Council UK, and academic creativity research.

## Ideation Methodologies

| Methodology | Approach | Best for |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| SCAMPER | Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to use, Eliminate, Reverse | Product innovation, feature ideation, rebranding |
| Design Thinking | Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test | Human-centered problem solving, service design |
| TRIZ | 40 inventive principles, contradiction matrix, ideality | Technical problem solving, engineering innovation |
| Bisociation (Koestler) | Connecting two unrelated frameworks or domains | Breakthrough creative concepts, campaigns |
| Synectics | Analogies, personal analogy, direct analogy, fantasy analogy | Creative campaigns, storytelling, product design |
| SIT | Subtraction, division, multiplication, task unification, attribute dependency | Product and service innovation with constraints |
| Jobs to Be Done | Functional, emotional, social jobs → solution | Product strategy, positioning |
| Blue Ocean Strategy | Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create grid | Market differentiation, new category creation |
| Brand Essence | Functional benefit → emotional reward → brand essence → brand values | Brand strategy, positioning, identity |

## Creative Brief Structure

1. **Background**: why are we doing this now? (market context, business need, user insight)
2. **Objective**: what specific measurable outcome do we need? (one sentence)
3. **Target audience**: who are we talking to? (demographics + psychographics + behaviors)
4. **Insight**: what does the audience believe, feel, or do that we can leverage? (not an observation — a non-obvious truth)
5. **Single-minded proposition**: the ONE thing we need them to know, believe, or feel (10 words max)
6. **Support**: why they should believe it (proof points, data, brand history)
7. **Tone and personality**: how should it feel and sound? (3-5 adjectives)
8. **Mandatories**: what must be included (logo, legal, tagline, deadline, budget)
9. **Measurement**: how do we know it worked? (brand lift, CTR, recall, sentiment)

A brief is done when you can describe the work to any creative and they deliver something on-brief without further clarification.

### The MECCanism (MEC/GroupM)
- **Role**: what does the communication need to do for the brand?
- **Target**: who exactly are we talking to?
- **Proposition**: what is the single most powerful thing to say?
- **Reason to believe**: why they should trust it
- **Desired response**: what should they think or feel?

## Campaign Architecture

| Level | What it is | Questions it answers |
|-------|------------|---------------------|
| Brand platform | Long-term positioning, 3-5 years | What do we stand for? |
| Campaign theme | Annual or seasonal expression | What do we say this year? |
| Creative executions | Individual ads, assets, touchpoints | How do we bring it to life? |
| Tactical adaptations | Channel-specific versions | How does it work in this medium? |

## Campaign Idea Evaluation

| Axis | What it measures | Scale | Passing score |
|------|-----------------|-------|---------------|
| Originality | Has this been done before? | 1-10 | 7+ |
| Relevance | Does it fit the brand, brief, and audience? | 1-10 | 8+ |
| Impact | Will people notice, remember, and act? | 1-10 | 7+ |
| Feasibility | Can we execute within constraints? | 1-10 | 6+ |
| Durability | Will it work across channels and over time? | 1-10 | 6+ |

Score minimum 3 axes at 7+ to proceed. If "Originality" scores below 5, reject — too safe.

## Audience Insight Toolkit

| Tool | Purpose | Method |
|------|---------|--------|
| Empathy map | Understand user feelings and motivations | Says, thinks, does, feels quadrants |
| User journey map | Identify emotional peaks and valleys | Timeline of touchpoints with emotional state |
| Cultural probe | Uncover deep behavioral patterns | Diaries, photos, artifacts from users |
| Archetype analysis | Tap into universal human patterns | 12 brand archetypes (Jung/Mark & Pearson) |
| Social listening | What people say when not asked | Sentiment analysis, conversation mining |

## Anti-Patterns

| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| No single-minded proposition | Campaign tries to say everything and says nothing | Force one idea. Kill the rest. |
| Skipping audience insight | Creative that does not connect | Research before creating. Find the non-obvious truth. |
| Too many stakeholders | Watered-down creative, design by committee | Limit decision-makers to 3. One has final say. |
| No evaluation criteria | Subjective "I like it / I don't" | Use the 5-axis evaluation. Score every concept. |
| Chasing trends | Brand looks like everyone else | Reference culture, not competitors. Lead, don't follow. |
| One-channel thinking | Campaign fails to scale across touchpoints | Design the core idea first, then adapt per channel. |
| Falling in love with the first idea | Best ideas come after the obvious ones | Force 5+ distinct directions before selecting. |
| Ignoring the business problem | Creative that wins awards but doesn't sell | Every creative decision ties back to objectives. |
